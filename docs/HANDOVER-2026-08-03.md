## 2. The geometry model — measured, visible, enforced

`mount.geometry` in the live config now describes the rig the operator actually owns, and the
two days' correction history says why none of it should be edited from memory again:

* **The head is a lettered chain** (`head:` in config; the letters are the project vocabulary):
  **A** RA∩DEC → **B** RA∩second boss (160 mm down the shaft — was "60" until the letters
  existed) → **C** the bearing joint (80 mm, ⊥ the shaft) → **D** the main boss's foot at 60° to
  the base — the tripod's centreline. The chain self-checks: its derived plate depth (~257 mm
  below A) has to agree with the measured `mount_body_height_mm` (250), and a >50 mm mismatch
  logs a warning naming both numbers.
* **Three moving capsules**: tube (bare 70 + margin = 80, lying *on* the saddle — the old 140 mm
  "saddle to centreline" was a tape run to the tube's far side, i.e. its diameter), the
  counterweight (350/75), and the camera rooted on the tube's *skin* at **F**, reaching 300 mm
  along +d̂ ("parallel to the dec axis, in front of the mount").
* **Two fixed obstacles besides the ground**: the tripod cone standing on **D**, and **H — the
  altitude knob** (70 mm reach, 25 mm radius, mid-boss, pointing north into the under-pier
  passage). H is enforced because the operator has already struck it; refusals name it.
* **Survey state** (`cargo run -p astroctl-safety --example rig_survey`): 0 % of the sky
  hard-refused, zenith clear, ~87° of the RA turn refused at dec-home — the genuine
  tube-under-pier passage — 14 % refused pier-unknown (irrelevant on hardware, which reports
  its side).

**The rig model viewer is the only sanctioned way to work on geometry**
(`tools/rig-viewer/rig-model-viewer.html`, published artifact
`https://claude.ai/code/artifact/b1400da4-714b-40e3-a867-94a0766ccc78` — republish via that URL
from new sessions). Free-text geometry debugging cost ~$500 before the viewer existed; with it,
the operator found the mount-axis offset, the boss lean and its direction, the A→B correction,
the saddle contact, F's true root, and three viewer-frame bugs — each in minutes. The workflow:
update the drawing, let the operator compare it with the metal, fix what they point at, only
then touch `rig.rs`. The viewer's math mirrors `rig.rs` line for line and must stay that way.

**The plumb line was hung (2026-08-03, end of session)**: A lands **3 cm north of the plate's
centre bolt** — so D is 30 mm *south* of A, where the chain derived it ~20 mm north. The
measurement now overrides the chain (`head.a_north_of_plate_mm: 30` in the live config,
mirrored in the viewer's plumb slider); the chain remains as the drawing and as a cross-check
the model logs when the two diverge. Resurveyed with the correction: pier-unknown refusals
dropped to 8.9 %, hard-blocked stays 0 %, zenith clear; the RA band at the pole pointing
widened (~81° bare tube, ~129° with the fattened capsule) because the cone truly stands closer
to the under-pier swing. Stage 2's windows can now be trusted to this geometry.

## 3. Safety semantics that landed (all hardware-provoked)

* **The singular pointing is solved**: drivers report the dec-axis bearing
  (`dec_axis_hour_angle_degrees`, = s·h, branch-free); the model tests the actual pose at the
  pole instead of sweeping all of them. Home is not a prison.
* **Every limit has an escape**: a colliding pose permits exactly the motions that are strictly
  shallower without descending; for RA commands the bearing the lookahead is judged at advances
  with the motion, so the two directions differ even at the pole. Both traps that forced this
  (altitude-floor overshoot at 13°, watch-overshoot parking 2° inside the band) are regression
  tests now.
* **Lookahead scales with speed**: 1° for rungs ≤32×, 2° at Fast, 6° at Max — 835× punched
  2–3° through the floor per press before this.
* **Gotos are collision-checked at the arrival pose**
  (`destination_bearing_degrees`, same `goto_solution` the goto runs). Altitude-only gotos are
  history. This is Stage 1 of the planner (§4).
* **The manual ladder is by mechanism**: 1/8/32× unbounded (every rung heard to start —
  E11+E16: the standing start is the wall, ~32×–39×, and the protocol forbids re-rating a
  running high-speed slew), Fast/Max are chained 30° firmware-ramped gotos cruising at the
  firmware's ≈51×/≈835×, `K` on release. A chunked rung self-terminates ≤30° after link loss.
* **Camera**: `capture.progress` has a terminal `failed` state (a refused shutter used to show
  "reading off the camera" forever), and both capture paths drop the body out of live view
  before firing (the R10's "I/O in progress" refusal). **Unverified on hardware**: the R10's
  PTP session was wedged when the operator last tried; power-cycle the body, connect, capture —
  live view on or off — and watch the interlock earn its keep.

## 4. Next milestone: the motion planner (semantics decided, Stage 1 shipped)

The operator decided: **the session's first goto auto-picks the pier side with the longest
uninterrupted window and reports it**; the window runs **until the first hard limit** (meridian,
altitude floor, collision band, target setting) — no configured duration. On a 2-axis GEM this
is exact enumeration: two candidate poses per target, and tracking advances only the bearing (at
the sidereal rate), so a whole night sweeps in milliseconds.

Stage 2, in order of teeth:

1. **Make the meridian watch pier-side-aware.** Today it stops tracking N minutes past the
   meridian regardless of side, so a pre-flipped start buys nothing. The un-flipped side is the
   dangerous one; the flipped side can track through for hours. Small code, careful review — it
   *loosens* a limit for one side.
2. **The window calculator** in `astroctl-safety`: per candidate branch, step the tracked night
   forward and return the first stop and its reason.
3. **Branch preference into the first goto.** `goto_solution` free-picks only at dec = 0 (from
   home); thread the planner's verdict there — optional HAL surface, same pattern as
   `pier_side`/the bearings.
4. **Report the plan** in the goto response; PWA shows "east side — 3 h 40 m before the
   meridian limit".

Stage 3 afterwards: sweep the goto's own trajectory against the model, with a two-leg waypoint
fallback when the direct path grazes the band or the knob.

## 6. Method notes these two days paid for

* **Never debug geometry in words.** The viewer exists because free text cost ~$500 and three
  days; a drawing plus the operator's eyes found ten model errors in an afternoon. Letters
  (A–H) are the shared vocabulary — extend the alphabet rather than describing positions.
* **The tape lies in one specific way**: three separate "radius" measurements arrived as
  diameters or far-side runs (tube 200→70, saddle 140→70, and the original top plate). When a
  number implies absurd hardware, it is the tape's anchoring, not the hardware.
* **Every limit needs an escape direction, proven by test.** Positional-without-escape bricked
  the mount twice in one day. The altitude check's "permit anything that climbs" was the
  template; collision needed penetration ordering *and* a bearing that moves with the command.
* **The counter is the instrument nobody uses**: it counts *commanded* steps, so during a stall
  or a buzz it testifies to what the controller believed — reading it settled E16 in minutes
  after an evening of guessing.
* **The operator's observation outranks the model, every time.** Ten for ten across two days.
